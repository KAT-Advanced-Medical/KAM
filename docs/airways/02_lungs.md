# Thorax Injuries & Pulmonary Support

## Diagnostic Indicators
* **SpO2 Decay:** Thorax trauma yields direct Oxygen Saturation (`SpO2`) loss independent of mechanical airway obstructions. 
* **Visual/Audio Cue:** Unconscious drop rates accelerate. Screen black flashing signals rapid desaturation. Conscious patients emit a distinct coughing sound every 30 seconds.

## Chest Inspection Outcomes
*Action performed directly on the chest.*

* **Chest rise and fall observed:** Regular spontaneous respiration.
* **No chest movement:** Complete respiratory arrest.
* **Uneven chest rise and fall:** Pneumothorax (PTX).
* **Chest sides are uneven:** Non-breathing state paired with PTX, Tension Pneumothorax (TPTX), or Hemothorax (HPTX).
* **Skin is rigid, looks bruised:** Hemothorax (HPTX) with blood accumulation.

## Advanced Thorax Injury Matrix
*Note: Auscultating lung sounds via Stethoscope twice increases diagnosis accuracy in Hardcore configurations.*

| Injury Type | Lung Audio Profile | Required Surgical / Field Equipment | Field Treatment Flow |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pneumothorax (PTX)** | Short Breath | Chest Seal, NCD Kit, or AAT Kit | Apply Chest Seal $\rightarrow$ Auscultate. If sound persists, execute Needle Decompression. |
| **Tension Pneumothorax (TPTX)** | Short Breath (Advanced) | Chest Seal, NCD Kit, or AAT Kit | Immediate Needle Decompression or AAT Kit decompression. |
| **Hemothorax (HPTX)** | Cracking Sound | Chest Seal, AAT Kit | Apply Chest Seal $\rightarrow$ Perform Fluid Draining utilizing AAT Kit $\rightarrow$ Re-auscultate. |

* **Deep Penetrating Wound (DPI):** Crossing specified weapon damage thresholds triggers internal thoracic trauma calculations with set random probabilities.
* **Pneumothorax Progression:** Develops dynamically across 4 distinct stages. Reaching Stage 4 or receiving secondary ballistic impacts triggers immediate transformation into TPTX or HPTX. Internal bleeding occurs under HPTX.

## Mechanical Oxygenation (BVM)
* **Apnea Mode:** Slows ongoing `SpO2` degradation when the patient is non-breathing.
* **Spontaneous Mode:** Accelerates internal `SpO2` recovery ratios.
* **Oxygenation Period Optimization:** Following 30 chest compressions (~18s), a dedicated tactical window opens where the Bag Valve Mask (BVM) functions with elevated conversion factors—raising oxygenation values even amidst active cardiac arrest.
* **Oxygen Integration:** Standard BVM units hook directly into Portable Oxygen Tanks (150L/300L sizes; refilled at vehicles/medical facilities). Medical facilities serve as infinite oxygen sources. Pocket BVMs are compact but cannot utilize oxygen attachments.
